Practical guides: understanding the result

A useful calculator is only part of a good decision. The examples below explain what to include, what can change and why a reference number should not be mistaken for a guarantee.

1. Estimating an EV charging session

Begin with the amount of energy you want to add, not simply the full battery capacity. For a basic estimate, multiply usable battery capacity by the difference between your starting and target charge percentages. Then divide the required energy by the effective charging power.

Example: A 60 kWh usable battery charged from 20% to 80% needs approximately 36 kWh. At a constant 7.4 kW, the ideal calculation is 36 ÷ 7.4 ≈ 4.9 hours.

Real sessions can take longer because of charging losses, temperature, power limits, battery management and changing charging rates. For DC fast charging, power often decreases as the battery fills. Check your vehicle and charger specifications before planning a trip around an estimate.

2. Calculating resale profit and margin

A sale price is not profit. Include the purchase price, marketplace and payment fees, shipping paid by you, packaging, repairs and any other direct expenses. Profit is the amount remaining after these costs. Profit margin compares that profit with the selling price.

Example: Sell an item for €90 after buying it for €50, paying €10 in fees, €7 for shipping and €2 for packaging. Profit is €21; profit margin is 21 ÷ 90 × 100 ≈ 23.3%.

This does not automatically account for taxes, returns, unsold inventory or the value of your time. Those can materially affect the outcome. Before listing, compare actual completed sales and current fees on the marketplace you intend to use.

3. Indicative prices are not live offers

Some tools generate reference values using built-in factors rather than retrieving current listings from a seller's database. Such a figure can help frame a question, but it cannot confirm that a specific product exists at that price or is available to purchase.

When researching a vehicle, part or second-hand item, verify the exact model, condition, compatibility, delivery costs, seller location and listing date. Similar product names can describe very different configurations. A low estimate may exclude accessories, taxes or shipping.

MicroWebLab labels locally calculated values as indicative and directs visitors to independent services for current information. A marketplace link is a starting point for verification, not a guarantee or endorsement of a particular offer.

4. How browser-based music discovery works

A music discovery interface can help organize searches and listening choices without storing copies of songs. TuneWiseALL uses YouTube services and a visible embedded player for available videos. Favorites, queues and playlists organize references and preferences within the application.

Playback still depends on YouTube, the rights holder, regional availability and network access. A video may become unavailable or restricted, and embedded playback may be subject to provider policies. The application does not promise access to every track.

If you use shared devices, remember that browser-stored preferences may remain accessible in the same browser profile. Use the application's controls or your browser's storage settings when you want to clear local information.

About MicroWebLab

MicroWebLab is a small independent project focused on making useful digital tasks more approachable. The aim is to offer working browser tools alongside explanations that help visitors interpret results responsibly.

What we build

Our current projects focus on EV charging research, resale calculations and YouTube-based music discovery. Rather than requiring an account to access the core web versions, the tools are designed to open directly in a modern browser.

Some personal features, such as favorites or inventories, may use local browser storage. Each app provides more specific information about its features and integrations.

How to use the information

Calculations depend on their inputs and assumptions. Estimates are not live quotes, certified valuations or professional advice. Third-party marketplaces, automotive services and YouTube are independent of MicroWebLab.

Before spending money or making a technical decision, verify prices, compatibility, safety requirements and availability with the original provider or a qualified professional where appropriate.

Frequently asked questions

Are the current MicroWebLab web apps free to open?

Yes. The current browser versions can be opened without creating a MicroWebLab account. Features or business models may change in the future, and relevant changes should be disclosed on the affected application.

Does MicroWebLab sell the products shown on marketplaces?

No. Marketplace and automotive links lead to independent services. MicroWebLab does not control their listings, prices, availability or transaction terms.

Are EV and resale estimates guaranteed?

No. Charging calculations depend on battery and charger conditions. Resale calculations depend on accurate costs and fees. Locally generated reference prices are indicative, not live commercial offers.

Can I use these tools on a phone?

The tools are browser-based and are intended for modern browsers. Individual features may depend on device support, network availability or third-party services.

Does TuneWiseALL store or download music files?

No. It uses YouTube services and embedded playback for available videos. YouTube and content owners control the underlying media and its availability.
